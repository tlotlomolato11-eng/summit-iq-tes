More area, more force: F = p × A

Pressure is the force on each square metre. Every extra patch of piston gets pushed too, so a bigger area means a bigger total force. Drag the area and count the patches.

A quantity of gas is trapped by a piston in a cylinder with thin metal walls. The piston is free to move without friction within the cylinder. The cylinder is placed inside a freezer.

(a) The air in the freezer is at atmospheric pressure, which is 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. The area of the piston in contact with the air in the freezer is 2.4 × 10⁻³ m².

(a)(i)Calculate the force exerted on the piston by the air in the freezer.[2]
(a)(ii)When the cylinder is first placed into the freezer, the temperature of the gas in the cylinder decreases and the air pushes the piston into the cylinder. Calculate the work done on the piston by the air in the freezer as the air pushes the piston a distance of 0.021 m into the cylinder.[2]
(b)The initial temperature of the cylinder and the gas is 21 °C and, in the freezer, the temperature of the cylinder decreases to –18 °C. The thermal capacity of the cylinder is 89 J / °C. Calculate the change in the internal energy of the cylinder.[2]
(c), (d)Particle explanations of the gas pressure and of the metal's smaller contraction.[3] [2]

ΔT answers “how many degrees?”. The question asks for a change in internal energy, which is measured in joules. The thermal capacity converts one into the other: 89 joules for every degree.

Slip #14(a)(i) · [2]
What you didDivided: F = p ÷ A. That gives about 4.2 × 10⁷ N, the weight of roughly 4000 tonnes, on a piston smaller than a phone screen.
What earns the marksp = F ÷ A rearranges to F = p × A = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 2.4 × 10⁻³ = 240 N. C1 for F = pA, A1 for 240 N.
Slip #24(b) · [2]
What you didFound the temperature change correctly, 21 − (−18) = 39 °C, and stopped there. That answers “how many degrees”, not “how many joules”.
What earns the marksE = C × ΔT = 89 × 39 = 3.5 × 10³ J, a decrease. C1 for E = CΔT or 89 × 39, A1 for 3.5 × 10³ J.
  • Also, 4(a)(ii): work done = force × distance was the right method and earns credit carried forward from your force. With the correct force it is 240 × 0.021 = 5.0 J.
