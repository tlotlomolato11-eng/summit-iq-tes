The rope isn't pulling straight down

The rope pulls at 60° to her body, so its moment arm is not the 1.2 m you can see. Drag the rope and watch the real moment arm, 1.2 × sin θ, change.

A rock climber, of total mass 62 kg, holds herself in horizontal equilibrium against a vertical cliff. She pulls on a rope that is fixed at the top of the cliff and presses her feet against the cliff.

(a)Calculate the total weight of the climber.[1]
(b)State the two conditions needed for equilibrium.[2]
(c)(i)The climber's centre of mass is 0.90 m from the cliff. Calculate the moment about her feet due to her weight.[2]
(c)(ii)The line of the rope meets the horizontal line through her centre of mass at a distance of 1.2 m from the cliff, as shown in Fig. 3.1. The rope is at an angle of 60° to the horizontal. Determine the tension in the rope.[3]

620 × 0.90 = 558 N m
d⊥ = 1.2 × sin θ
Tension needed, T
If you divide by 1.2 m

The rope's pull only turns her about her feet through the perpendicular distance from the feet to the rope's line. Only at θ = 90° does that equal the full 1.2 m. At any other angle it is shorter, so the tension has to be bigger to balance the same 558 N m.

What you didUsed g = 9.8 and wrote the weight in kg.
What earns the markThis paper says to take 1.0 kg as weighing 10 N. W = mg = 62 × 10 = 620 N. Weight is a force, so its unit is N. Mass is in kg.
Slip #23(c)(ii) · [3]
The gapTreating 1.2 m as the rope's moment arm. The rope is at 60°, not 90°, so the 1.2 m is not perpendicular to it. Dividing by 1.2 gives 465 N, which is too small.
What earns the marksT × 1.2 sin 60° = 560, so T = 560 ÷ (1.2 × sin 60°) = 540 N. C1 for using any moment, C1 for T × 1.2 sin 60°, A1 for 540 N.
