The strips become magnets

Close switch S. The coil's current magnetises the two soft-iron strips. Their facing ends become opposite poles, opposite poles attract, and the strips snap together to complete the motor circuit. There is no induced current anywhere in this process.

Slip #17(a) · [3]
What you wroteThe strips attract because of an “induced current”. Nothing is being induced here. The coil's current is steady, and the strips are just iron.
What earns the marksX and Y become magnetised (B1). Their ends in the centre are opposite poles, so X and Y attract (B1). They touch and close the switch, completing the motor circuit (B1).
Slip #27(b)(ii) · [2]
What you wroteThick wires are used to increase resistance. That is backwards.
What earns the marksA large cross-section gives a smaller resistance (B1). With a current as large as 150 A, that means less thermal energy is produced, so the wires don't overheat or melt (B1).
  • Also, 7(b)(i): I = P ÷ V was right, but the power was ten times too big, giving 1500 A. 1.8 kW is 1800 W, so I = 1800 ÷ 12 = 150 A.
  • Also, 7(c): your answer was about high-voltage power transmission, which is a different topic. The question wants the motor circuit drawn: the strips (W to Z), the motor and the battery all in series, as in the diagram above.
