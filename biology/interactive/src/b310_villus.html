Check #1Q3.10(a), (b) · [3] [2]

Stop the blood flow and watch the gradient. Why does a rich blood supply matter? Then switch to fats: why the lacteal?

Mark points(a) Blood flowing through the capillaries continuously carries absorbed glucose and amino acids away, keeping their concentration inside the villus low. This maintains a steep concentration gradient between the gut contents and the blood, so diffusion keeps happening efficiently instead of slowing as concentrations equalise.
(b) Fats are not water soluble. Inside the villus cells they are reformed into tiny fat droplets, which pass more easily into the larger, more permeable lacteal (lymph) vessels than directly into the blood capillaries.
Check #2Q3.10(c), (d) · [4] [3]

Switch the liver to “damaged”. Name two blood changes and explain each. Why can't amino acids simply be stored?

Mark points(c) Any two, each explained. Raised blood amino acid or ammonia levels, because the damaged liver cannot deaminate excess amino acids properly, so they are not converted to (and removed as) urea. Poorer blood glucose regulation, because the liver cannot store excess glucose as glycogen as effectively, so blood glucose may swing more after eating. Build-up of toxic substances the liver would normally detoxify.
(d) The body has no dedicated storage molecule for amino acids: proteins are structural and functional, not an energy store. Excess amino acids go to the liver, which deaminates them to form urea (excreted by the kidneys) and converts the rest into a form used for energy or turned into glycogen or fat.
