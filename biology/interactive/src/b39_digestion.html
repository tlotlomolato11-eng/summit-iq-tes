Smaller pieces, faster enzymes

Chewing changes the size of food, not what it is made of. That is mechanical digestion. Enzymes break big molecules into small ones, which is chemical digestion. Chew, and watch amylase work faster on the bigger surface. Then swallow and let protease and acid take over in the stomach.

Check #1Q3.9(b), (d) · [3] [3]

Run the enzymes for 1 minute without chewing, then try again after chewing twice. Why is the second run faster? Then describe the whole mouthful using both terms.

Mark points(b) Chewing breaks food into many smaller pieces, increasing its surface area. A larger surface area means enzymes can contact and act on more of the food at once, speeding up chemical digestion.
(d) Chewing is mechanical digestion: it physically breaks food into smaller pieces without changing it chemically, increasing surface area. Saliva (containing amylase) begins chemical digestion in the mouth, breaking down starch. After swallowing, mechanical churning and chemical digestion both continue further along the alimentary canal.
Check #2Q3.9(a), (c) · [3] [1]

Switch to the stomach. What does protease do, and where does it act? What else does the acid do?

Mark points(a) Protease breaks down proteins into amino acids. It acts in the stomach (as pepsin) and in the small intestine (from the pancreas).
(c) The acid kills bacteria or pathogens that may be present in the food.
