Check #1Q3.4(a) · [4]

Switch between the two solutions at 6 weeks. Why is one pattern about the veins and the other about the age of the leaf?

Mark pointsMagnesium deficiency reduces chlorophyll production throughout the leaf, but the veins keep some chlorophyll a little longer, giving yellowing between the veins. Nitrate deficiency causes protein and chlorophyll breakdown first in older leaves, because the plant moves (translocates) scarce nitrogen out of old leaves toward new growth. That is an age-based pattern, not a veins-versus-between-veins one.
Check #2Q3.4(b), (d) · [2] [3]

Why keep the complete-solution jar? And why does missing nitrate mean less growth?

Mark points(b) It acts as a control, showing what normal growth looks like. Without it you can't be sure any difference is caused by the missing nitrate rather than some other factor.
(d) Nitrate is needed to make amino acids, which are built into proteins. Proteins are essential for making new cells (structural proteins, enzymes). Without enough nitrate the plant cannot build new tissue as fast, so growth (height, leaf number) is reduced.
