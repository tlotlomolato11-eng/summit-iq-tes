(a) This reaction is catabolic: amylase breaks a large molecule (starch) into smaller ones (maltose). An anabolic reaction builds large molecules from small ones, e.g. joining amino acids to form a protein.

Check #1Q3.6(b) · [4]

Run A at 5 °C and B at 80 °C. Both break down little starch. Give the different reason for each.

Mark pointsAt 5 °C the enzyme has very little kinetic energy, so few successful enzyme–substrate collisions occur and little starch is broken down. At 80 °C the enzyme is denatured: its active site's shape is permanently destroyed by the heat, so it can no longer bind starch at all. Little or no starch is broken down, but for a different reason.
Check #2Q3.6(c) · [3]

After the run, set both tubes to 35 °C and run again. Which one recovers, and why?

Mark pointsWarming the 5 °C tube to 35 °C (near the optimum) increases the rate, because the enzyme was undamaged and simply moving slowly. Cooling the 80 °C tube to 35 °C does NOT restore activity, because that enzyme was already denatured at 80 °C. The change to its shape is permanent, so the rate stays low.
