Limiting factor

A limiting factor is whichever condition is in shortest supply. Raising it speeds the reaction up. Raising anything else does nothing.

Check #1Q3.3(c) · [3]

Why does extra CO₂ speed photosynthesis up, and why not forever? Try the CO₂ button at high light, then at low light.

Mark pointsMore CO₂ provides more raw material, so more glucose can be made per unit time and the rate increases. Eventually another factor (light intensity or temperature) becomes limiting instead, so adding further CO₂ beyond that point makes no more difference.
Check #2Q3.3(d) · [3]

Drag the light from zero to full. Describe the curve and name the limiting factor on the flat part.

Mark pointsRate increases roughly in proportion to light intensity at first, then levels off (plateaus) at high light intensity. At that point light is no longer limiting. The rate is now limited by CO₂ concentration or temperature instead.
