Hooke's Law: the spring constant

Hang a mass on a spring and it stretches. Hooke's law says the stretch grows in direct proportion to the force, as long as you stay inside the spring's elastic limit. The spring constant k tells you how stiff the spring is.

Key ideas

F
The force stretching the spring, in newtons (N). Here it is the weight of the mass: F = m × g, with g = 9.81 m/s².
x
The extension: how far the spring stretches beyond its natural (resting) length, in metres.
k
The spring constant in N/m: the force needed to stretch the spring by one metre. Bigger k means a stiffer spring. On the graph, k is the slope of the line.
Elastic limit
The largest stretch a spring can take and still spring back to its original length. Past it, the spring deforms permanently and F = kx no longer holds, so the graph curves.
Energy
A stretched spring stores elastic potential energy E = ½ k x², the area under the F–x line.
Oscillation
Pull the mass down and let go: it bounces with period T = 2π√(m/k). Stiffer springs and lighter masses bounce faster.
